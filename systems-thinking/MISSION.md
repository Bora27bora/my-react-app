# Mission: Thinking in Systems

## Why
The user is reading *Thinking in Systems: A Primer* (Donella Meadows) and wants to use it to make better personal/life decisions — recognizing the recurring patterns (habits, routines, relationships, finances) in their own life as systems, so that short-term fixes stop quietly backfiring.

## Success looks like
- Can take a real pattern from their own life and name its elements, interconnections, and purpose — and notice when the "real" purpose (deduced from behavior) differs from the stated one.
- Can sketch a basic causal loop / stock-and-flow diagram for a personal situation, including loop polarity (reinforcing vs. balancing).
- Can recognize a "system trap" (e.g. Fixes that Fail, Shifting the Burden, Escalation) playing out in their own life and name a leverage point that addresses it instead.
- Can recall the book's core vocabulary (stock, flow, feedback loop, delay, leverage point, etc.) from memory, not just recognize it.

## Constraints
- Pace lessons alongside the user's own reading — check which chapter they're currently on rather than racing ahead.
- Mix practice styles across lessons: applying concepts to real personal situations, diagramming (causal loops / stocks & flows), and short retrieval-practice quizzes. Vary which is used; let what works guide future sessions.

## Out of scope
- Formal system dynamics software / modeling (Vensim, STELLA, etc.) — not needed for personal decision-making.
- Deep dives into large-scale organizational or societal systems, beyond what the book itself covers.
